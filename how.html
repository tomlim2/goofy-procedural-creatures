how it is drawn

MENAGERIE is a board of thirty-odd creatures grown from one seed — human, cat, dog and imp, each breathing on its own clock, drawn as if by hand. The same seed gives the same board every time, and it runs in the browser with no build step: the board is here, the code is here.

This page is the medium — what the creatures are drawn with, on three axes: the goofy outline — what a creature's lines are drawn with — the goofy material — how its surfaces are filled — and the goofy fur — how its hair is grown — and the shapes and colours it is all made of. No textures, no brush images: the hand comes from rules. It is all three.js (WebGL) and there is no 2D canvas anywhere — every stroke and every fill is triangles, one geometry per layer, and the boil is a set of pre-drawn variants shown in turn.

Every figure below is drawn live by the code that draws the creatures — the same src/stroke.js and src/medium/, at the board's own scale — so nothing here can drift from what the board does. The figures hold still; INK BOIL in the header (or I) sets their lines boiling at the board's own pace.

THE GOOFY OUTLINE

Every line on a creature is a goofy outline — a kind from GOOFY_OUTLINES in src/medium/outlines.js. A part names no kind, no line function and no width. It names its line's role: ink.contour(path, { color, weight }) closes a shape and ink.line(…) runs open, down to a dot — with at most a weight on the kind's width (a head's contour runs at 1.15 of a body's). One switch, BOARD_LINES, says which kind draws each role; change a name there and every line of that role changes.

A kind is named the pen, then the hold. One pen is on the table, the pencil, in three holds: STROKE (once, full width — mass) · SLINE (thin, the pen lifts — detail) · BROKEN (three times over itself — contour). One line each, the same path:

in use on the board

The two rows read the switch live: 45 contours and 119 open lines, at quantized weights, so no creature wears a two-line ear beside a one-line eye. The only strokes outside the table are the two bands — a hat's colour laid as a thick stroke, a fill in disguise.

contour — 45: the head (weight 1.15), the bodies, the ears (all kinds, and the cat's and dog's), the helmet, cap and brim, beret and pot, the horn tip, the hands, boots and sleeves, the tail's tuft, pom and beads, the hair loops, bun and twintail balls, the eye rings and eyewear, the nose, the mouth's grid, tongue and fangs, the static eyes, the star and heart eyes — fine (0.7) for the eyes and the mouth's small parts, heavy (1.2) for the hats. line — 119: lids, brows, lashes and the eyes' marks, whiskers, tears, the nose's lines, eyewear arms, lips, teeth rows and tongues, limbs and feet, tail spines and rings, hair strands, ties and spikes, hat seams and brims' ink, horns, side ears and the folded ear's root and crease, decal edges, the floor, the ? glyph, the rig's shut and smiling lids, and the dots and dashes — the teeth strip's edges, the mouth grid's bars, the grin's fangs, the dot mouth, the boot's toe lines, a tail's rings and spines, a pom's claw, a beret's nub, the philtrum, the sleeping z z — fine 0.6 for the thinnest, 1.3 for lids, muzzle lines and horns, 1.6 for a stub tail.

the pencil — PENCIL_STROKE · PENCIL_SLINE · PENCIL_BROKEN

The spine wanders on two sines per world length, the width breathes on two more, the ends run past where they stop instead of pinching, and a line thick enough sheds: ink crumbs on the edge, paper-coloured bites inside. A closed pencil line is seamless — its sines snap to whole cycles and it never overshoots. Every number is in PENCIL in src/stroke.js. The ink is opaque, which is what lets the creature in front hide the one behind.

Three kinds are this one pencil, told apart by the hold. PENCIL_STROKE is full width, laid once: every contour and every open line on the board. PENCIL_SLINE is thinner, and the pen lifts about once every 0.26 of a unit for a hair — never within 0.025 of an end, and not at all below 0.07 of length, so a dot or a dash keeps its whole extent. PENCIL_BROKEN is laid three times over itself, each pass wandering and breathing on its own. SLINE and BROKEN are built and on nothing; the switch above says what the board draws.

A dot or a dash needs no kind of its own. The pencil keeps the ends of anything shorter than 0.05 and sheds nothing there: an overshoot would run a dot half again to twice as long, and a crumb is the size of the dot. A dot is a line that came out short, and nothing else.

One habit per row, each row the row above plus one. The path handed in is straight, so everything a row shows is the habit it adds. The last row is the whole pencil:

the ribbon pen — off the table

WebGL draws no thick line, so nothing here is a line: every stroke is a filled triangle ribbon. Four habits have to be present together — even re-sampling, noise along the normal (a slow bend and a fine tremor), an end taper, uneven pressure through the middle. Drop one and it reads as clip art. The ribbon pen is no kind and no role draws with it; two things still call it directly — a hat's band and an emoji's glyph outline. Its taper goes to nothing at both ends, so it draws nothing shorter than its 0.03 re-sample step; stubs are the pencil's.

The same, one habit per row:

both pens

Both take their noise from the individual's own hand, and both come out of the same triangle buffer: a stroke is a chain of quads. Two samples make four corners — each sample pushed to both sides along its normal by the half width — and the four are cut into two triangles. A 350 px line is about 117 of them. Where the two pens part company is the normal: the ribbon pen takes one per segment, so at a corner the two quads meet at different angles and the outside opens a wedge; the pencil takes one per point and hands it to the quads on both sides, so the corner closes. On a closed pencil line the last quad joins the first, which is why it has no seam.

THE GOOFY MATERIAL

A goofy material is what a surface is made of, the way a 3D material is — how its area is filled, as channels. (Goofy, like the outline and the fur, to keep it apart from the GPU material underneath.) There are two channels and they paint the same thing, the colour of the surface:

base — the fill-up: always opaque, printed out of register, in the part's colour. It carries the creature's pattern (stripes, dots, spots, hatching) inside it, clipped to the contour, the way a pattern is part of an albedo, and its decals — colour regions that take their edge from the host's own outline, like a calico's patches — so the texture passes over them.
texture — hatch, scratch, dab or speckle, laid over the base and clipped to the contour.

That is the whole of it. There is no opacity channel; grain — the paper showing through — is not a channel at all but the sheet's, done for the whole board in the paper's pass (below); and the contour is the goofy outline, above. The colour always comes from the part, and every tone the texture adds is a shade of that colour. A part names a material and hands over the path and the colour — fills.paint(path, "FLAT", { color, offset }) — and never picks a technique itself.

On the board a creature has one: its material slot (FLAT for most; GRAPHITE, INK, OIL or CHARCOAL for the rest) fills its head, its body and everything else it fills — ears and tail, eyes and nose, teeth and hat — each at its own colour's step. Two more slots ride along: density, the hand (light, normal, heavy), and pattern, drawn inside the base colour. A material also carries its tooth — how the paper bites it: graphite rides the grain and skips it (0.45), charcoal dusts it (0.4), flat 0.3, ink soaks in (0.12), oil buries it (0.06) — scaled by how hard the hand pressed.

The table is GOOFY_MATERIALS, and every entry below gets a row of shader balls: the same ball in the same colour at the five value steps — black · hatch · scribble · stipple · light, named for the way graphite makes each. A material renders darkness its own way: graphite changes technique step by step, ink, oil and charcoal lay down more or less of their texture. A step is in the colour first and the marks second — it pulls the base toward the technique's own tone (graphite and charcoal darken it, ink lightens it: its scratches take the ink away) and lays the marks on top, because a mark fine enough to be pretty up close falls under a device pixel on a board and takes the whole scale with it. On the board the step comes from the part's colour — a dark cloth draws black, a pale skin light — moved one step by the creature's density.

A mark has to be lighter than what it is drawn on or it is not a mark, so on a dark ground every technique turns around, lays its marks light and pulls the colour the other way. That is the lower row of each pair. The balls are drawn on a card rather than on the paper, so they show each fill with no bite taken out of it; the board is where the paper comes through.

the paper, the GPU material and the boil

Underneath there is exactly one GPU material — a flat MeshBasicMaterial with vertex colours, shared per opacity level by every mesh on the board. No depth test, so stacking is decided entirely by renderOrder, and a layer's fills and ink are baked into one geometry (fills first, ink after — inside a mesh, vertex order is the stacking): one draw call per layer.

The paper is the board's one shader — a procedural GLSL grain on a single plane, no texture and no tile (src/scene/paper.js). The frame is two passes: the board on a transparent target, then the sheet over the canvas with the board on it.

The paper shows through the drawing, fills included — not in that composite but in every mark. Each one carries its own bite (its material's tooth at its value's press) and the ink shader mixes it toward the sheet's colour where the grain peaks. A colour mix, not a hole, so the board stays opaque within itself and the creature in front still hides the one behind.

the colors

Only the ones that hold up on paper; push the saturation and the hand-drawn feel breaks immediately. And they go in as linear: three.js reads vertex colors as linear space, so an sRGB hex fed raw brightens — the ink #2b2724 would come out a mid grey. Every hex passes through hexToRgb → srgbToLinear in src/color.js, nothing bypasses it.

THE GOOFY FUR

Hair and fur are neither a contour nor a filling — in 3D they are a groom, and here they are the goofy fur: how hair is grown along a path, named from GOOFY_FUR in src/medium/fur.js — ink.fur(path, "SCRIBBLE", { color, passes, spread }). A part hands over the path (the crown arc, a tail, a bang) and the color; passes, width and spread ride as a style's volume, everything else is the fur's own. One fur stands in the table: SCRIBBLE, and every hairstyle is grown with it. The same ball, fur grown along its crown:

SCRIBBLE — the scribble

The same path drawn over and over. Each pass is pushed outward from the root (root −0.25 → reach 0.6 spreads, so the mass fans away from the face), every point waves on its own, and a share of each push leans sideways.

THE SHAPES

A head is not a circle but a lumpy mass. One recipe, blobPath, makes every closed shape on the board: a superellipse bent by noise, squared and tapered by two knobs.
