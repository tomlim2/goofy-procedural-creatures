MENAGERIE HOW IT IS DRAWN · ← GRID BOOT

Everything on the board is made of what is on this page, on three axes: the goofy outline — what a creature's lines are drawn with — the goofy material — how its surfaces are filled — and the goofy fur — how its hair is grown. No textures, no brush images: the hand comes from rules. It is all three.js (WebGL) and there is no 2D canvas anywhere — every stroke and every fill is triangles, one geometry per layer, and the boil is a set of pre-drawn variants shown in turn. Every figure here is drawn live by src/stroke.js and src/medium/, the same code that draws the creatures. The figures hold still; INK BOIL in the header (or I) sets their lines boiling at the board's own pace.

THE GOOFY OUTLINE

Every line on a creature is a goofy outline — a kind from GOOFY_OUTLINES in src/medium/outlines.js. A part names no kind, no line function and no width. It names its line's role: ink.contour(path, { color, weight }) closes a shape, ink.line(…) runs open, ink.mark(…) is a dot or a dash a few widths long — with at most a weight on the kind's width (a head's contour runs at 1.15 of a body's). One switch, BOARD_LINES, says which kind draws each role; change a name there and every line of that role changes.

A kind is named the pen, then the hold. Two pens — the pencil and the ribbon pen — and three holds: STROKE (once, full width — mass) · SLINE (thin, the pen lifts — detail) · BROKEN (three times over itself — contour). Four kinds stand in the table. One line each, the same path:

The three rows read the switch live: 44 contours, 104 open lines and 14 marks, at quantized weights, so no creature wears a two-line ear beside a one-line eye. The only strokes outside the table are the two bands — a hat's colour laid as a thick stroke, a fill in disguise.

contour — 44: the head (weight 1.15), the bodies, the ears (all kinds, and the cat's and dog's), the helmet, cap and brim, beret and pot, the horn tip, the hands, boots and sleeves, the tail's tuft, pom and beads, the hair loops, bun and twintail balls, the eye rings and eyewear, the nose, the mouth's grid, tongue and fangs, the static eyes, the star and heart eyes — fine (0.7) for the eyes and the mouth's small parts, heavy (1.2) for the hats. line — 104: lids, brows, lashes and the eyes' marks, whiskers, tears, the nose's lines, eyewear arms, lips, teeth rows and tongues, limbs and feet, tail spines and rings, hair strands, ties and spikes, hat seams and brims' ink, horns, side ears and the folded ear's root and crease, decal edges, the floor, the ? glyph, the rig's shut and smiling lids — fine 0.6 for the thinnest, 1.3 for lids, muzzle lines and horns, 1.6 for a stub tail. mark — 14: freckles, the philtrum, the dot mouth, teeth's edges and fang bumps, toes and claws, a hat's knob, the ! glyph.

the pencil — PENCIL_STROKE · PENCIL_SLINE · PENCIL_BROKEN

The spine wanders on two sines per world length, the width breathes on two more, the ends run past where they stop instead of pinching, and a line thick enough sheds: ink crumbs on the edge, paper-coloured bites inside. A closed pencil line is seamless — its sines snap to whole cycles and it never overshoots. Every number is in PENCIL in src/stroke.js. The ink is opaque, which is what lets the creature in front hide the one behind.

Three kinds are this one pencil, told apart by the hold. PENCIL_STROKE is full width, laid once: every contour and every open line on the board. PENCIL_SLINE is thinner, and the pen lifts about once every 0.26 of a unit for a hair — never within 0.025 of an end, and not at all below 0.07 of length, so a dot or a dash keeps its whole extent. PENCIL_BROKEN is laid three times over itself, each pass wandering and breathing on its own. SLINE and BROKEN are built and on nothing; the switch above says what the board draws.

One habit per row, each row the row above plus one. The path handed in is straight, so everything a row shows is the habit it adds. The last row is the whole pencil:

the ribbon pen — RIBBONPEN_STROKE

WebGL draws no thick line, so nothing here is a line: every stroke is a filled triangle ribbon. Four habits have to be present together — even re-sampling, noise along the normal (a slow bend and a fine tremor), an end taper, uneven pressure through the middle. Drop one and it reads as clip art. RIBBONPEN_STROKE is this stroke laid once, and it draws every mark: short, it comes out a bean, which is what a mark wants.

Both take their noise from the individual's own hand, and both come out of the same triangle buffer.

THE GOOFY MATERIAL

A goofy material is what a surface is made of — the way a 3D material is (goofy, like the outline and the fur, to keep it apart from the GPU material underneath): how its area is filled, as channels. The base colour — the fill-up — always opaque and printed out of register, in the part's color, carrying the creature's pattern (stripes, dots, spots, hatching) inside it, clipped to the contour, the way a pattern is part of an albedo — and its decals, colour regions that take their edge from the host's own outline (the calico's patches), painted in the base too, so the texture passes over them; and its texture — hatch, scratch, dab or speckle — the medium's pattern laid over it, clipped to the contour. Both paint the same thing, the colour of the surface. There is no opacity channel; grain — the paper showing through — is not a channel at all but the sheet's, done for the whole board in the paper's pass (below). That is the goofy material, and nothing else: the contour is the goofy outline, above. The color always comes from the part, and every tone the texture adds is a shade of that color. A part names one and hands over the path and the color — fills.paint(path, "FLAT", { color, offset }) — and never picks a technique itself. On the board each creature's head and body take the creature's goofy material (the material slot: FLAT for most, GRAPHITE, INK, OIL or CHARCOAL for the rest) — and so does everything it fills, ears and tail, eyes and nose, teeth and hat: one tool per creature, the details at their own colour's step — at its density (another slot: a light hand, a normal one, a heavy one); its pattern (stripes, dots, spots, hatching — a third slot) is drawn inside the base colour. A material also carries its tooth — how the paper bites it: graphite rides the paper's grain and skips it (0.45), charcoal dusts it (0.4), flat 0.3, ink soaks in (0.12), oil buries it (0.06), scaled by how hard the hand pressed. The balls below are drawn on a card rather than on the paper, so they show each fill with no bite taken out of it; the board is where you see the paper come through. The table is GOOFY_MATERIALS in src/medium/materials.js; every entry gets a row of shader balls — the same ball in the same color at the five value steps, black · hatch · scribble · stipple · light, named for the way graphite makes each: a goofy material renders darkness its own way, graphite changing technique step by step, ink, oil and charcoal laying down more or less of their texture. A step is in the colour first and the marks second — it pulls the base toward the technique's own tone (graphite and charcoal darken it, ink lightens it: its scratches take the ink away) and lays the marks on top, because a mark fine enough to be pretty up close falls under a device pixel on a board and takes the whole scale with it. On the board the step comes from the part's color — a dark cloth draws black, a pale skin light — moved one step by the creature's density, its hand. A mark has to be lighter than what it is drawn on or it is not a mark, so on a dark ground every technique turns around and lays its marks light and the step pulls the colour the other way — the lower row of each pair is the same five steps on a dark ground.

the GPU material underneath, and the boil

Underneath there is exactly one GPU material — a flat MeshBasicMaterial with vertex colors, shared per opacity level by every mesh on the board. The paper under it is the board's one shader — a procedural GLSL grain on a single plane, no texture and no tile (src/scene/paper.js, drawing.md § the paper) — and the frame is two passes: the board on a transparent target, then the sheet over the canvas with the board on it. The paper shows through the drawing, fills included — not in that composite but in every mark: each one carries its own bite (its material's tooth at its value's press) and the ink shader mixes it toward the sheet's colour where the grain peaks. A colour mix, not a hole, so the board stays opaque within itself and the creature in front still hides the one behind. No depth test, so stacking is decided entirely by renderOrder; a layer's fills and ink are baked into one geometry (fills first, ink after — within a mesh, vertex order is the stacking), one draw call per layer.

the colors

Only the ones that hold up on paper; push the saturation and the hand-drawn feel breaks immediately. And they go in as linear: three.js reads vertex colors as linear space, so an sRGB hex fed raw brightens — the ink #2b2724 would come out a mid grey. Every hex passes through hexToRgb → srgbToLinear in src/color.js, nothing bypasses it.

THE GOOFY FUR

Hair and fur are neither a contour nor a filling — in 3D they are a groom, and here they are the goofy fur: how hair is grown along a path, named from GOOFY_FUR in src/medium/fur.js — ink.fur(path, "SCRIBBLE", { color, passes, spread }). A part hands over the path (the crown arc, a tail, a bang) and the color; passes, width and spread ride as a style's volume, everything else is the fur's own. One fur stands in the table: SCRIBBLE, and every hairstyle is grown with it. The same ball, fur grown along its crown:

SCRIBBLE — the scribble

The same path drawn over and over. Each pass is pushed outward from the root (root −0.25 → reach 0.6 spreads, so the mass fans away from the face), every point waves on its own, and a share of each push leans sideways.

THE SHAPES

A head is not a circle but a lumpy mass. One recipe, blobPath, makes every closed shape on the board: a superellipse bent by noise, squared and tapered by two knobs.
