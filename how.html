MENAGERIE HOW IT IS DRAWN · ← GRID BOOT

Everything on the board is made of what is on this page, on three axes: the goofy outline — what a creature's contour is drawn with — the goofy material — how its surfaces are filled — and the goofy fur — how its hair is grown. No textures, no brush images; the hand comes from rules alone. And it is all three.js (WebGL) — there is no 2D canvas anywhere: every stroke and every fill is triangles, one geometry per layer, and the boil is a set of pre-drawn variants shown in turn. Every figure is drawn live by src/stroke.js and src/medium/, the same code that draws the creatures. The figures hold still; INK BOIL in the header (or I) sets their lines boiling at the board's own pace.

THE GOOFY OUTLINE

Every line on a creature is drawn with a goofy outline — a kind from GOOFY_OUTLINES in src/medium/outlines.js. A part never names a kind, a line function or a width: it names its line's role — ink.contour(path, { color, weight }) for the closed line of a shape, ink.line(…) for an open line, ink.mark(…) for a dot or a dash a few widths long — with at most a weight on the kind's width (the head's contour runs at 1.15 of the body's). Which kind each role is drawn with is one switch, BOARD_LINES: change a name there and every line of that role on the board changes; a new kind is a new entry in the table and a name in the switch. Four kinds exist today. A kind is named the pen, then how it is held — PENCIL_ or RIBBONPEN_, and STROKE (once, at full width: mass) · SLINE (thin, and the pen lifts: detail) · BROKEN (laid three times over itself: contour), the three holds the reference keeps its line in. in use on the board

What the board draws each role with today — the three rows read the switch live. RIBBONPEN_STROKE — the ribbon pen

WebGL cannot draw a thick line, so there are no lines here at all — every stroke is a triangle ribbon. Four things have to be present together to read as a pencil: re-sampling at an even spacing, noise along the normal (a slow bend plus a fine tremor), an end taper, and uneven pressure through the middle. Leave out any one and it becomes vector clip art. RIBBONPEN_STROKE is this stroke, once — the board's first line, and still what every mark is drawn with.

PENCIL_STROKE · PENCIL_SLINE · PENCIL_BROKEN — the pencil, held three ways

The reference's line (kindergrimm § the pencil), kept next to the ribbon as s.pencil. The spine wanders on two sines per length, the width breathes, the ends run past where they should stop instead of pinching, and a thick line sheds — ink crumbs on the edge, paper bites inside. Theirs is a polygon filled on a 2D canvas; ours is the same polygon as three.js triangles, and its 62% ink is left out on purpose (ours stays opaque — an opaque fill is what lets a front creature hide the one behind it), and so is its tremor. Every number is in PENCIL in src/stroke.js. A closed pencil line is seamless: its sines snap to whole cycles and it never overshoots.

Three of the four kinds are this one pencil, told apart by how it is held. PENCIL_STROKE is it at full width, laid once — the contour and the open line of everything on the board. PENCIL_SLINE is it held light: thinner, and the pen lifts about once every 0.26 of a unit, leaving the line open for a hair — never within 0.025 of an end, and not at all on a line shorter than 0.07, so a dot or a dash keeps its whole extent. PENCIL_BROKEN is it laid three times over itself, each pass wandering and breathing on its own, the way a hand going round twice leaves a contour. PENCIL_SLINE and PENCIL_BROKEN are built and on nothing yet — the switch above says what the board actually draws.

THE GOOFY MATERIAL

A goofy material is what a surface is made of — the way a 3D material is (goofy, like the outline and the fur, to keep it apart from the GPU material underneath): how its area is filled, as channels. The base colour — the fill-up — always opaque and printed out of register, in the part's color, carrying the creature's pattern (stripes, dots, spots, hatching) inside it, clipped to the contour, the way a pattern is part of an albedo — and its decals, colour regions that take their edge from the host's own outline (the calico's patches), painted in the base too, so the texture passes over them; and its texture — hatch, scratch, dab or speckle — the medium's pattern laid over it, clipped to the contour. Both paint the same thing, the colour of the surface; a channel that would be a different thing — opacity (the reference's 62% graphite) — is not built yet; grain (the paper showing through) is not a channel at all but the sheet's, done for the whole board in the paper's pass (below). That is the goofy material, and nothing else (the contour is the goofy outline, above). The color always comes from the part, and every tone the texture adds is a shade of that color. A part names one and hands over the path and the color — fills.paint(path, "FLAT", { color, offset }) — and never picks a technique itself. On the board each creature's head and body take the creature's goofy material (the material slot: FLAT for most, GRAPHITE, INK, OIL or CHARCOAL for the rest) — and so does everything it fills, ears and tail, eyes and nose, teeth and hat: one tool per creature, the details at their own colour's step — at its density (another slot: a light hand, a normal one, a heavy one); its pattern (stripes, dots, spots, hatching — a third slot) is drawn inside the base colour. A material also carries its tooth — how the paper bites it: graphite rides the paper's grain and skips it (0.45), charcoal dusts it (0.4), flat 0.3, ink soaks in (0.12), oil buries it (0.06), scaled by how hard the hand pressed. The balls below are drawn on a card rather than on the paper, so they show each fill with no bite taken out of it; the board is where you see the paper come through. The table is GOOFY_MATERIALS in src/medium/materials.js; every entry gets a row of shader balls — the same ball in the same color at the five value steps, black · hatch · scribble · stipple · light (the reference's scale, named for the way graphite makes each): a goofy material renders darkness its own way, graphite changing technique step by step, ink, oil and charcoal laying down more or less of their texture. A step is in the colour first and the marks second — it pulls the base toward the technique's own tone (graphite and charcoal darken it, ink lightens it: its scratches take the ink away) and lays the marks on top, because a mark fine enough to be pretty up close falls under a device pixel on a board and takes the whole scale with it. On the board the step comes from the part's color — a dark cloth draws black, a pale skin light — moved one step by the creature's density, its hand. A mark has to be lighter than what it is drawn on or it is not a mark, so on a dark ground every technique turns around and lays its marks light and the step pulls the colour the other way — the lower row of each pair is the same five steps on a dark ground.

the GPU material underneath, and the boil

Underneath there is exactly one GPU material — a flat MeshBasicMaterial with vertex colors, shared per opacity level by every mesh on the board. The paper under it is the board's one shader — a procedural GLSL grain on a single plane, no texture and no tile (src/scene/paper.js, drawing.md § the paper) — and the frame is two passes: the board on a transparent target, then the sheet over the canvas with the board on it. The paper shows through the drawing, fills included — not in that composite but in every mark: each one carries its own bite (its material's tooth at its value's press) and the ink shader mixes it toward the sheet's colour where the grain peaks. A colour mix, not a hole, so the board stays opaque within itself and the creature in front still hides the one behind. No depth test, so stacking is decided entirely by renderOrder; a layer's fills and ink are baked into one geometry (fills first, ink after — within a mesh, vertex order is the stacking), one draw call per layer.

the colors

Only the ones that hold up on paper; push the saturation and the hand-drawn feel breaks immediately. And they go in as linear: three.js reads vertex colors as linear space, so an sRGB hex fed raw brightens — the ink #2b2724 would come out a mid grey. Every hex passes through hexToRgb → srgbToLinear in src/color.js, nothing bypasses it.

THE GOOFY FUR

Hair and fur are neither a contour nor a filling — in 3D they are a groom, and here they are the goofy fur: how hair is grown along a path, named from GOOFY_FUR in src/medium/fur.js — ink.fur(path, "SCRIBBLE", { color, passes, spread }). A part hands over the path (the crown arc, a tail, a bang) and the color; passes, width and spread ride as a style's volume, everything else is the fur's own. One fur exists: SCRIBBLE, what every hairstyle is grown with today. The same ball, fur grown along its crown:

SCRIBBLE — the scribble

The same path drawn over and over. Each pass is pushed outward from the root (root −0.25 → reach 0.6 spreads, so the mass fans away from the face), every point waves on its own, and a share of each push leans sideways. Candidates not grown yet: TUFT (short and dense — an animal's tail end), WISP (thin and sparse), CLUMP (strands that bunch, the groom's clump).

THE SHAPES

The reference's head is not a circle but a lumpy mass. One recipe, blobPath, makes every closed shape on the board: a superellipse bent by noise, squared and tapered by two knobs.
