how it is drawn

MENAGERIE is a board of thirty-odd creatures grown from one seed — human, cat, dog and imp, each breathing on its own clock, drawn as if by hand. The same seed gives the same board every time, and it runs in the browser with no build step: the board is here, the code is here.

This page is the medium — what they are drawn with, on three axes: the goofy outline, the goofy material and the goofy fur, plus the shapes and colours. No textures and no brush images; the hand comes from rules. It is all three.js, every stroke and fill a triangle.

Every figure below is drawn live by the code that draws the creatures — the same src/stroke.js and src/medium/, at the board's own scale, so nothing here can drift from what the board does. INK BOIL (or I) sets them boiling; a reload draws them all in another hand.

THE GOOFY OUTLINE

Every line on a creature is a goofy outline — a kind from GOOFY_OUTLINES in src/medium/outlines.js. A part names no kind, no line function and no width. It names its line's role: ink.contour(path, { color, size }) closes a shape and ink.line(…) runs open, down to a dot — with at most a size on it. One switch, BOARD_LINES, says which kind draws each role; change a name there and every line of that role changes.

A kind is named the pen, then the hold. One pen is on the table, the pencil, in three holds: STROKE (once, full width — mass) · SLINE (thin, the pen lifts — detail) · BROKEN (three times over itself — contour). One line each, the same path:

A kind also draws at a size — S · M · L, each kind naming its own three: STROKE 0.004 · 0.007 · 0.012, SLINE 0.002 · 0.003 · 0.005, BROKEN 0.0035 · 0.006 · 0.011. A ladder is the kind's own rather than a multiple of one width, because a hairline blown up to STROKE's L stops being a hairline. A size is the only way a width is said — no multiplier on top, so a width that is not on a ladder cannot be drawn. Most lines are M, fine detail S, and two lines on the whole board L. The hold does not change with the size: SLINE still lifts at L, BROKEN still stacks its ghosts at S. AGAIN redraws in another hand.

in use on the board

The two rows read the switch live: 45 contours and 119 open lines, at three widths and no others, so no creature wears a two-line ear beside a one-line eye. What the table does not cover: the two bands (a hat's colour laid as a thick stroke, a fill in disguise), the emoji glyphs, and the marks a goofy material and the goofy fur lay down — the medium's own, not a creature's lines. All of them the same pencil.

contour — 45 of them: the head and bodies, the ears, hats and hands, the eyes and eyewear, the nose, the mouth's parts. line — 119: lids, brows and lashes, whiskers, limbs and feet, hair strands, horns, the floor, and every dot and dash. S for the finest, M for the rest, L for an imp's horn and a stub tail.

the pencil

A stroke is no line — WebGL draws none thick enough. It is a chain of quads: two samples make four corners, each pushed to both sides along its normal by the half width, cut into two triangles. The pencil takes one normal per point and hands it to the quads on both sides, so a corner closes; on a closed line the last quad joins the first, which is the seam it does not have.

PENCIL_STROKE · PENCIL_SLINE · PENCIL_BROKEN — one pencil, three holds. Its sines run per world length rather than per line, so the wander comes out the same size wherever it lands; a closed line snaps them to whole cycles and never overshoots, which is the rest of why it has no seam. Every number is in PENCIL in src/stroke.js. The ink is opaque, which is what lets the creature in front hide the one behind.

PENCIL_STROKE is full width, laid once — every line on the board. PENCIL_SLINE is a hairline whose pen lifts: the skips are in world units along the path as handed over, so the kind breaks in the same places at every size, and none falls near an end or on a line too short to spare it. PENCIL_BROKEN stacks the ghost: two thinner passes, one at a fifth of the ink and one at half, each slipping to one side or the other, with the line laid last on top. SLINE and BROKEN are built and on nothing — the switch says what the board draws.

A dot or a dash needs no kind of its own: the pencil keeps the ends of anything shorter than 0.05 and sheds nothing there, because an overshoot would run a dot half again to twice as long and a crumb is the size of the dot.

One habit per row, each row the row above plus one. The sixth is the whole pencil — that is PENCIL_STROKE; the seventh adds the ghost, and stacking that is PENCIL_BROKEN.

THE GOOFY MATERIAL

A goofy material is what a surface is made of, the way a 3D material is — how its area is filled, as channels. (Goofy, like the outline and the fur, to keep it apart from the GPU material underneath.) There are two channels and they paint the same thing, the colour of the surface:

base — the fill-up: always opaque, printed out of register, in the part's colour. It carries the creature's pattern (stripes, dots, spots, hatching) inside it, clipped to the contour, the way a pattern is part of an albedo, and its decals — colour regions that take their edge from the host's own outline, like a calico's patches — so the texture passes over them.
texture — hatch, scratch, dab or speckle, laid over the base and clipped to the contour.

That is the whole of it — no opacity channel, and grain is the sheet's job, not a material's. A part names a material and hands over the path and the colour (fills.paint(path, "FLAT", { color, offset })); it never picks a technique, and every tone the texture adds is a shade of the part's colour.

A creature has one, its material slot — FLAT for most, GRAPHITE, INK, OIL or CHARCOAL for the rest — and it fills everything the creature fills. Two slots ride along: density, the hand, and pattern.

Every entry in GOOFY_MATERIALS gets a row of shader balls — the same ball at the five value steps, black · hatch · scribble · stipple · light, named for the way graphite makes each. A step is in the colour first and the marks second: it pulls the base toward the technique's own tone and lays the marks on top, because a mark fine enough to be pretty up close falls under a device pixel on a board. The step comes from the part's colour, moved one by the creature's density.

A mark has to be lighter than what it is drawn on, so on a dark ground every technique turns around — the lower row of each pair.

the paper, the GPU material and the boil

Underneath there is exactly one GPU material, a flat MeshBasicMaterial with vertex colours shared by every mesh. No depth test, so renderOrder decides all the stacking, and a layer's fills and ink bake into one geometry — one draw call per layer.

The paper is the board's one shader (src/scene/paper.js): procedural GLSL grain on a single plane, no texture and no tile, drawn behind everything in the same pass. It shows around the drawing, not through it: the ink and the fills are opaque, which is what lets the creature in front hide the one behind.

the colors

Only the ones that hold up on paper; push the saturation and the hand-drawn feel breaks immediately. And they go in as linear: three.js reads vertex colors as linear space, so an sRGB hex fed raw brightens — the ink #2b2724 would come out a mid grey. Every hex passes through hexToRgb → srgbToLinear in src/color.js, nothing bypasses it.

THE GOOFY FUR

Hair is neither a contour nor a filling — in 3D it is a groom, here the goofy fur: ink.fur(path, "SCRIBBLE", { color, passes, spread }), named from GOOFY_FUR. A part hands over the path and the colour; passes, width and spread ride as a style's volume and the rest is the fur's own. One fur stands in the table, SCRIBBLE, and every hairstyle is grown with it:

SCRIBBLE — the scribble

The same path drawn over and over, each pass pushed further out from the root so the mass fans away from the face, every point waving on its own and a share of each push leaning sideways.

THE SHAPES

A head is not a circle but a lumpy mass. One recipe, blobPath, makes every closed shape on the board: a superellipse bent by noise, squared and tapered by two knobs.
