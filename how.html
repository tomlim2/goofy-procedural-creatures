Everything on the board is made of what is on this page. No textures, no brush images — the hand comes from rules alone. Every figure below is drawn live by src/stroke.js, the same code that draws the creatures. The figures hold still; INK BOIL in the header (or I) sets their lines boiling at the board's own pace.

THE LINE

WebGL cannot draw a thick line, so there are no lines here at all — every stroke is a triangle ribbon. Four things have to be present together to read as a pencil: re-sampling at an even spacing, noise along the normal (a slow bend plus a fine tremor), an end taper, and uneven pressure through the middle. Leave out any one and it becomes vector clip art.

And what an area gets. A flat fill is allowed, but it must miss its outline a little — and anything that means shading is not a fill at all, it is the pen going back and forth.

THE MATERIAL

There is exactly one material — a flat MeshBasicMaterial with vertex colors, shared per opacity level by every mesh on the board. No depth test, so stacking is decided entirely by renderOrder; a layer's fills and ink are baked into one geometry (fills first, ink after — within a mesh, vertex order is the stacking), one draw call per layer.

Its colors — only the ones that hold up on paper; push the saturation and the hand-drawn feel breaks immediately. And they go in as linear: three.js reads vertex colors as linear space, so an sRGB hex fed raw brightens — the ink #2b2724 would come out a mid grey. Every hex passes through hexToRgb → srgbToLinear in src/color.js, nothing bypasses it.

THE SHAPES

The reference's head is not a circle but a lumpy mass. One recipe, blobPath, makes every closed shape on the board: a superellipse bent by noise, squared and tapered by two knobs.
