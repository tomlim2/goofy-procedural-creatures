MENAGERIE HOW IT IS DRAWN · ← GRID BOOT

Everything on the board is made of what is on this page, on three axes: the goofy outline — what a creature's contour is drawn with — the material — how its surfaces are filled — and the goofy fur — how its hair is grown. No textures, no brush images; the hand comes from rules alone. Every figure is drawn live by src/stroke.js and src/medium/, the same code that draws the creatures. The figures hold still; INK BOIL in the header (or I) sets their lines boiling at the board's own pace.

THE GOOFY OUTLINE

A creature's contour is drawn with one goofy outline, named from GOOFY_OUTLINES in src/medium/outlines.js — ink.contour(path, "PENCIL", { color, closed, weight }). A part never picks a line function or a width; at most a weight (the head's contour runs at 1.15 of the body's). Two outlines exist: PENCIL, the contour of the head and the body, and RIBBON, the line of every other closed shape — ears, hats, hands, eyes, noses, mouths — at one width, scaled by a part's weight. One line each, the same path:

in use on the board

What the board's closed lines are actually drawn with today — two kinds, both through the table. Forty-one outline() calls at eight widths, eleven of them laid twice, became one RIBBON at one width with three weights (fine 0.7 · 1 · heavy 1.2); a creature no longer wears two-line ears beside one-line eyes. What is left to decide is which of those shapes should move from RIBBON to PENCIL.

pencil — 3 parts: the head (weight 1.15), the biped body, the quad body. ribbon — 41: the ears (all kinds, and the cat's and dog's), the helmet, cap and brim, beret and pot, the horn tip, the hands, boots and sleeves, the tail's tuft, pom and beads, the hair loops, bun and twintail balls, the eye rings and eyewear, the nose, the mouth's grid, tongue and fangs, the static eyes, the star and heart eyes — fine (0.7) for the eyes and the mouth's small parts, heavy (1.2) for the hats.

RIBBON — the ribbon

WebGL cannot draw a thick line, so there are no lines here at all — every stroke is a triangle ribbon. Four things have to be present together to read as a pencil: re-sampling at an even spacing, noise along the normal (a slow bend plus a fine tremor), an end taper, and uneven pressure through the middle. Leave out any one and it becomes vector clip art. RIBBON is this stroke, once.

PENCIL — the pencil

The reference's line (kindergrimm § the pencil), kept next to the ribbon as s.pencil. The spine wanders on two sines per length, the width breathes, the ends run past where they should stop instead of pinching, and a thick line sheds — ink crumbs on the edge, paper bites inside. Its 62% ink is left out on purpose (ours stays opaque), and so is its tremor. Every number is in PENCIL in src/stroke.js. A closed pencil line is seamless: its sines snap to whole cycles and it never overshoots.

THE MATERIAL

A material is what a surface is made of — the way a 3D material is: how its area is filled, as channels. The base colour — the fill-up — always opaque and printed out of register, in the part's color, carrying the creature's pattern (stripes, dots, spots, hatching) inside it, clipped to the contour, the way a pattern is part of an albedo — and its decals, colour regions that take their edge from the host's own outline (the calico's patches), painted in the base too, so the texture passes over them; and its texture — hatch, scratch, dab or speckle — the medium's pattern laid over it, clipped to the contour. Both paint the same thing, the colour of the surface; a channel that would be a different thing — opacity (the reference's 62% graphite), grain (the paper showing through) — is not built yet. That is the material, and nothing else (the contour is the goofy outline, above). The color always comes from the part, and every tone the texture adds is a shade of that color. A part names one and hands over the path and the color — fills.paint(path, "FLAT", { color, offset }) — and never picks a technique itself. On the board each creature's head and body take the creature's material (a slot: FLAT for most, GRAPHITE, INK, OIL or CHARCOAL for the rest) — and so does everything it fills, ears and tail, eyes and nose, teeth and hat: one tool per creature, the details at their own colour's step — at its density (another slot: a light hand, a normal one, a heavy one); its pattern (stripes, dots, spots, hatching — a third slot) is drawn inside the base colour. The table is MATERIALS in src/medium/materials.js; every entry gets a row of shader balls — the same ball in the same color at the five value steps, black · hatch · scribble · stipple · light (the reference's scale, named for the way graphite makes each): a material renders darkness its own way, graphite changing technique step by step, ink, oil and charcoal laying down more or less of their texture. On the board the step comes from the part's color — a dark cloth draws black, a pale skin light — moved one step by the creature's density, its hand.

the material underneath, and the boil

Underneath there is exactly one GPU material — a flat MeshBasicMaterial with vertex colors, shared per opacity level by every mesh on the board. No depth test, so stacking is decided entirely by renderOrder; a layer's fills and ink are baked into one geometry (fills first, ink after — within a mesh, vertex order is the stacking), one draw call per layer.

the colors

Only the ones that hold up on paper; push the saturation and the hand-drawn feel breaks immediately. And they go in as linear: three.js reads vertex colors as linear space, so an sRGB hex fed raw brightens — the ink #2b2724 would come out a mid grey. Every hex passes through hexToRgb → srgbToLinear in src/color.js, nothing bypasses it.

THE GOOFY FUR

Hair and fur are neither a contour nor a filling — in 3D they are a groom, and here they are the goofy fur: how hair is grown along a path, named from GOOFY_FUR in src/medium/fur.js — ink.fur(path, "SCRIBBLE", { color, passes, spread }). A part hands over the path (the crown arc, a tail, a bang) and the color; passes, width and spread ride as a style's volume, everything else is the fur's own. One fur exists: SCRIBBLE, what every hairstyle is grown with today. The same ball, fur grown along its crown:

SCRIBBLE — the scribble

The same path drawn over and over. Each pass is pushed outward from the root (root −0.25 → reach 0.6 spreads, so the mass fans away from the face), every point waves on its own, and a share of each push leans sideways. Candidates not grown yet: TUFT (short and dense — an animal's tail end), WISP (thin and sparse), CLUMP (strands that bunch, the groom's clump).

THE SHAPES

The reference's head is not a circle but a lumpy mass. One recipe, blobPath, makes every closed shape on the board: a superellipse bent by noise, squared and tapered by two knobs.
